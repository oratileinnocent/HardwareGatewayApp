The Clever Engineering Trick
Fooling the Computer: "I'm Just a USB Mouse"
SLIDE 03 / 12

🔌 What the Computer Thinks

When you plug a normal Dell or HP mouse into a computer, it works instantly. It never asks for a password or asks to install software.

My device does the exact same thing: It pretends to be a normal, physical USB mouse and keyboard. The computer accepts it unconditionally.

📱 What is Actually Happening

Inside the little box is a powerful microchip with its own built-in private Wi-Fi.

It broadcasts a private Wi-Fi signal to your phone. When you swipe or type on your phone screen, the little box whispers to the PC: 'The user just clicked the mouse' or 'The user just typed a letter.'

The Result: Zero software. Zero drivers. Zero traces left behind. Works on Windows, Mac, Linux, and even during computer startup menus!
🎙️ What to Say: "So instead of using software, I built a tiny physical gadget. When you plug it into any computer, the computer gets completely fooled in a good way: it thinks I just plugged in a regular USB mouse and keyboard from Dell. It doesn't ask for permission and requires no software. It broadcasts its own private Wi-Fi directly to my phone, and my phone becomes the mouse and keyboard."

Engineering Common Sense
Why Separate Them? "Smart Division of Labor"
SLIDE 05 / 12

❌ The Bad Way: Putting Video on the USB Stick

  • Microcontrollers have tiny processors and little memory.
  • Compressing video makes the microchip overheat and choke.
  • The mouse cursor starts lagging, jumping, and freezing.
  • If the video crashes, the whole mouse stops working!

✔️ My Way: Smart Division of Labor

  • The computer's powerful processor handles the video snapshots (The Eyes).
  • The tiny USB stick stays 100% focused on moving the mouse at lightning speed (The Hands).
  • The Big Win: Even if the video stops, your hands NEVER stop working! The mouse and keyboard continue to work flawlessly.
🎙️ What to Say: "Someone might ask: 'Why didn't you just make the tiny USB stick do the video streaming too?' Think of it like a smart division of labor: if you make the tiny USB stick do heavy video broadcasting, it gets hot, runs out of memory, and the mouse starts to lag. By letting the computer handle the video camera, the USB stick stays 100% focused on lightning-fast mouse glide. And even if the video is closed, your hands never stop working."

Physical Construction
The Gadget: Circuit Board & 3D-Printed Box
SLIDE 06 / 12

🔧 Inside the Box (The Circuit)

  • ESP32-S3 Microchip: The brain with dual processors and native USB pins.
  • Vero Board Carrier: Clean soldered wiring with clearance channels for pins.
  • Two LED Traffic Lights:
    • Blue LED: Blinks when waiting; stays solid when phone connects.
    • Amber LED: Flashes every time a click or keystroke happens.

🖨️ Outside the Box (The 3D Casing)

  • Designed by me in 3D CAD (OpenSCAD) and printed in tough PETG plastic.
  • Pocket-sized snap-fit design that protects the electronics.
  • Internal light tubes so the blue and amber LED colors don't bleed together.
  • A comfortable push-button on top for the emergency kill-switch.

Built-In Safety
The Panic Button: Instant Physical Safety Cutoff
SLIDE 09 / 12

🛡️ Not a Hacking Tool

Some people hear 'USB gadget' and think of hacker tools like a Rubber Ducky.

My device is strictly an administrative bridge: It has zero automatic hacking scripts. It only clicks or types when a human being actively touches the phone screen.

🚨 The Physical Emergency Button

See that little push-button on top of the box? That is a physical Kill-Switch.

If you ever suspect someone is trying to connect without permission, you press the button. In less than 2 milliseconds, all wireless signals die completely like pulling an emergency brake cord.
